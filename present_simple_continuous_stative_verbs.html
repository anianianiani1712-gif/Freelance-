Present Simple vs Present Continuous

Stative verbs, dynamic meanings, common traps — and one big mixed test. Read the rule, practise in short rounds, then check whether you can choose the tense by meaning.

1. The core contrast

Present Simple

State, fact, habit, general truth

I understand the question now.
She has a new car.
This soup tastes delicious.

Present Continuous

Action in progress, temporary behaviour, arrangement

Tom is thinking about moving.
We are having dinner at eight.
He is being rude today.

Ask yourself: Is the verb describing a state (what someone/something is, feels, owns, knows) or an action/process happening now or around now?

2. Common stative verbs

These verbs are usually not used in continuous forms when they describe a state. Meaning matters: some can be dynamic in another context.

Mind / thoughts
know, understand, believe, think (= have an opinion), realise, remember, suppose, mean
Feelings / preferences
like, love, hate, prefer, want, need
Senses / appearance
see, hear, smell, taste, sound, seem, appear (= seem), look (= seem)
Possession / relation
have (= possess), belong, own, cost, fit

Examples: “I see what you mean.” / “I’m seeing the dentist tomorrow.” · “She looks tired.” / “She’s looking at the menu.”

3. The textbook examples

Present Simple = state / general meaning

I think he’s Italian. (= believe)
Katie looks happy. (= appears to be)
You can see the sea from my room. (= it is visible)
Derek has a new car. (= possesses)
This pie tastes really good.
This new dress fits her perfectly.
He is so polite. (= character)
She appears to be tired. (= seems)

Present Continuous = action / temporary meaning

Tom is thinking of moving house. (= considering)
They are looking at the paintings. (= taking a look)
Sam is seeing his friends tonight. (= meeting)
We are having dinner at eight. (= eating / arranged plan)
Paul is tasting the soup to see if it needs salt. (= trying it)
He is fitting a new lock on the door. (= attaching / installing)
He is being so rude today! (= behaving this way today)
She is appearing in a new TV show. (= taking part)

Same verb, different meaning

Choose the tense based on the meaning in brackets.

1. I (think) you’re right. = have an opinion

2. I (think) about changing jobs. = consider

3. This perfume (smell) wonderful. = has a wonderful smell

4. Why (you / smell) the milk? = check its smell

5. She (have) two younger brothers. = family relationship

6. We (have) lunch with Maya right now. = eat

7. You (be) very impatient today! = temporary behaviour

8. The soup (taste) too salty. = its flavour

Mini reference: watch out for these

see
I see your point. (= understand)
I’m seeing my tutor tomorrow. (= meeting)
look
You look tired. (= seem)
Why are you looking at me? (= direct your eyes)
appear
He appears nervous. (= seems)
She’s appearing in a play. (= performing)
fit
The jacket fits me. (= is the right size)
He’s fitting a new kitchen. (= installing)
