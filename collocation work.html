(1) Ancient political philosophy means ancient Greek and Roman thought the period of Greek thought in the fifth century BCE to the end of the Roman empire in the fifth century CE. In academic literature this period is also referred to as “Classical antiquity”. Ancient political thought focused on the nature and political organization of society, the forms of political rule, the relationship between political life and . Greek philosophers Plato and Aristotle are considered the greatest intellectual figures of Classical antiquity.

(2) Aristotle (384 bce—322 bce) was an ancient Greek philosopher and scientist, one of the greatest intellectual figures of Classical antiquity and Western history. “Man is a ,” Aristotle observes. The aim of the Politics, Aristotle says, is to investigate, what makes for good government and what makes for bad government. Aristotle asserts that all communities aim at some good. The state (polis) is the highest kind of community, aiming at the highest of goods. Only within a state can human beings . Government, Aristotle says, must be in the hands of one, of a few, or of the many; and governments may govern for the or for the good of the rulers. Government by a single person for the general good is called “monarchy”; for private benefit, “.” Government by a minority is “aristocracy” if it aims at the state’s best interest and “oligarchy” if it benefits only the ruling minority. Popular government in the common interest Aristotle calls “polity”; he reserves the word “democracy” for anarchic mob rule.

B. POLITICAL THOUGHT IN THE MIDDLE AGES

Medieval political philosophy in Western Europe covers the period from the 5th to the 15th century. It aimed to Greek and Roman classical philosophy (primarily, the works by Plato and Aristotle) with the medieval theories focusing on theological issues, metaphysics, and the faith and reason . Thus, Western medieval thought was concerned with the boundaries between Church and State, and the related controversy over secular power and divine law. The most influential political thinkers of this period were St. Augustine and Thomas Aquinas.

C. RENAISSANCE POLITICAL PHILOSOPHERS

The Renaissance is a historical term used to describe a period of dramatic cultural, artistic, economic, and political changes in the life of medieval Europe. It started from the beginning of 14th century and lasted until the end of 17th century. Academic literature describes this period as a "rebirth" in Europe, the core characteristics of which were a revival of interest in classical antiquity, the rise of humanism, revolution in arts, and industrial and scientific innovations. The Renaissance gave birth to such brilliant intellectuals and political thinkers as Niccolò Machiavelli (1469—1527), Thomas Hobbs(1588–1679), John Locke (1632–1704), and many others.

Niccolò Machiavelli (1469—1527) was a Renaissance political thinker and the author of The Prince, a treatise that had laid the foundations of modern political science describing politics as . Machiavelli was the first philosopher to have claiming that the ultimate goal of any ruler was to maintain the stability and security of his rule. Another contribution to political science made by Machiavelli was the classification of rulers into lions and foxes. This political metaphor contrasted those who relied on power per se with those who used deception and cunning to achieve their goals. Centuries later this "lions and foxes theory" was expanded by the sociologist Vilfredo Pareto.

The English philosopher Thomas Hobbs (1588–1679) is best-known for his political theory focusing on the concept of "social contract". In the Leviathan, Hobbs described the life of human beings as "" and assumed that the only way for them to survive was to subject themselves to a strong government in the form of social contract. After Hobbs, as a party to the social contract, people should to a higher authority (the State) in exchange for security, order, and self-preservation. For Hobbs, order was the ultimate political good.
